Privacy policy

Last updated: November 3, 2026

The short version

Your secrets stay on your device. I can't see them, and neither can anyone else. UCrypt has no server of mine, no account system, and no analytics.

What UCrypt stores, and where

Your vault — every password, note, and setting — lives in your browser's own storage on your computer. It is encrypted with your master key before it touches the disk. If you uninstall the extension, that data goes away with it.

The optional Google Drive backup

Cloud sync is off by default. If you turn it on:

  • UCrypt asks Google for permission using Google's own sign-in screen, right in your browser. The permission is limited to the files UCrypt creates — it is not a pass to your whole Google account.
  • One encrypted copy of your vault is saved to your Drive. It is encrypted on your device before it is uploaded, so the file sitting on Google's servers is unreadable without your key.
  • Only you can read that file. I can't. Google can't. That is the whole point.

You can revoke the permission any time from your Google Account page (myaccount.google.com/permissions), and you can delete the backup file from your Drive like any other file.

What UCrypt never does

  • No accounts, no sign-ups, no server of mine receiving your data
  • No analytics, no trackers, no ads
  • No selling, sharing, or "anonymized" harvesting of anything

Recovery words

When you set up sync, UCrypt shows you recovery words so you can get back in from another device. Those words are your master key in readable form. Write them on paper and keep them somewhere safe — not in the vault itself. If you lose the words and lose your devices, nobody can restore your vault. Not even me. That is what "only you hold the key" means, and I think it is worth the trade.

Changes to this policy

If anything here changes, I'll update this page and the date at the top. If a change is big, it will also be announced in the extension's release notes.

          Security & autofill protection

          Security posture: OWASP 2023 PBKDF2 · Auto-lock active · Origin-strict autofill

          Primary security controls (Master passphrase and Auto-lock) stay open below. Advanced autofill rules, site exceptions, Sync Doctor, and the Danger Zone are organized into collapsible sections.

          Master passphrase

          Your master passphrase encrypts every entry in the vault with AES-256-GCM (PBKDF2-SHA256, 600,000 iterations — OWASP 2023). It is never sent anywhere, never stored in plaintext, and cannot be recovered if lost.

          Auto-lock

          Lock the vault automatically after a period without activity — shorter intervals are safer. Applies immediately, no restart needed.

          10 minutes (default) is the recommended balance — short enough to protect you, long enough to stay out of your way.

          Autofill behavior

          Your saved passwords get auto-filled in web forms. Also, you can automatically sign-in to websites where this functionality exists.

            Passkeys & sign-in methods

            What passkeys are — and why UCrypt never claims them.

            Some sites let you sign in with a passkey — a credential your browser or operating system creates and stores, unlocked by your device's own screen lock. UCrypt is not a passkey provider: it does not create, store, import or fill passkeys, and it will never claim to. Keep using your browser's built-in passkey manager for those sign-ins — UCrypt manages your passwords, 2FA seeds and other secrets alongside them.

            If a site offers "Sign in with a passkey" or "Use your fingerprint, face or screen lock", that prompt comes from your browser or the site itself — accept it there, in your browser's own flow. UCrypt's job stays the same: strong unique passwords and TOTP codes for everything that is not a passkey.

                                      Wordlist Pools & Diceware Entropy Reference
                                      
          How EFF Large, Short #1/#2, and custom wordlists compute per-word entropy bits.
                                              
        
                                      
          
                                        
            
                                      • EFF Large (7,776 words · 12.92 bits/word): Five-dice roll pool curated by the Electronic Frontier Foundation for maximum memorability and zero prefix collisions.
                                        • 
            
                                      • EFF Short #1 & #2 (1,296 words · 10.34 bits/word): Four-dice roll pools optimized for rapid mobile typing and distinct 3-character prefixes.
                                        • 
            
                                      • Custom Device Wordlist (log₂(N) bits/word): Extracted deterministically on-device from your chosen URL, pasted text, or .json list. Stored in chrome.storage.local and never synced to the cloud.
